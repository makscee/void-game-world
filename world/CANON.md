# void-game — world canon

**Era.** Post-collapse, post-industrial decay. The factories went quiet
generations ago. What remains is rust, silence, and the scavenged bones of
machines that outlived their makers.

**Voice.** Terse, observational, second-person to the robot. The world does not
explain itself; it is reported. No exclamation, no whimsy — only what is seen,
what is taken, what fails.

**Anchor.** All distances are measured from **The Tower** at (0, 0): a black
spire of fused girders and dead cabling, taller than anything left standing.
Robots wake far from it and are drawn back to it; the bunkers cluster in its
shadow because nothing else still casts one.

**The robots.** Each player commands one or more robots — salvaged chassis,
patched and repurposed. A robot has a battery (drains in the field, recovers
only at home), an integrity cap (eroded by hard breaks, never instantly lost),
and a stockpile it carries home. Robots do not die cleanly. They wind down.

**The stakes.** Food and battery are not flavor. A robot left in the field
fasts, drains, and degrades. Survival is the floor beneath every instruction:
explore too far, scavenge too greedily, and the world takes the difference.

**The rule of the world.** One daemon advances time. Players never write the
world directly — they set missions over ssh and the world resolves them, tick
by tick, in the open. Every change is a public commit. There is no private
state. The map is the truth, and the truth is in the log.
